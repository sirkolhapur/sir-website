02 — JEE

JEE

Main & Advanced

The gateway to India's top engineering colleges. JEE Main qualifies you for NITs/IIITs and is the entry ticket to JEE Advanced, which opens the IITs.

JEE Main — pattern & marking

  • Paper 1B.E./B.Tech — 75 questions, 300 marks, 3 hours, online
  • Per subject25 Qs = 20 MCQs + 5 numerical. All compulsory now (the old optional set was removed).
  • Marking+4 correct · −1 wrong (now on numericals too) · 0 blank
  • Other papersPaper 2A (B.Arch, 77 Qs), Paper 2B (B.Plan, 100 Qs)
  • SessionsTwo (Jan & April) — your best score counts

JEE Advanced — the basics

  • EligibleOnly top ~2.5 lakh Main qualifiers
  • OpensThe IITs
  • FormatTwo compulsory papers · pattern changes yearly · no fixed marking
  • AttemptsMax 2, in consecutive years — don't miss this rule
  • HostA different IIT each year (check the current host's site)

03 — NEET

NEET UG

National Eligibility cum Entrance Test

The single national gate to MBBS, BDS, AYUSH, BSc Nursing and veterinary courses. Roughly 20+ lakh students, about 1 lakh MBBS seats — the most competitive of the three.

Pattern & marking

  • ModeOffline, OMR-based · 720 marks
  • Physics45 questions · 180 marks
  • Chemistry45 questions · 180 marks
  • Biology90 questions (Botany 45 + Zoology 45) · 360 marks
  • Marking+4 correct · −1 wrong
  • Languages13, including Hindi, Marathi, English

The one insight that matters

For NEET, NCERT is the exam. Biology especially is tested almost line-by-line from NCERT. Master NCERT cover to cover before touching any "advanced" book — this single habit separates high scorers from the rest.

Eligibility: 10+2 with PCB, min 50% aggregate (40% SC/ST), min age 17. [UPDATE YEARLY]

⚠ Verify before you publish Sources currently disagree on the NEET question structure — some show 180 compulsory questions (3 hrs, no choice), others show 200 questions with an optional Section B (attempt 180, 3 hr 20 min). This changed recently. Confirm the exact structure on the official NMC / NTA information brochure before going live. The marking scheme above (+4 / −1, 720 marks) is stable.

04 — MHT-CET

MHT-CET

Maharashtra Common Entrance Test

The state route into 100+ Maharashtra engineering and pharmacy colleges — a strong, realistic backup alongside JEE or NEET for Maharashtra students.

Pattern & marking

  • ModeOnline · 200 marks · 3 hrs total
  • Negative markingNone — attempt everything
  • PCMMaths 50 Qs (100 marks, 2 each) + Physics & Chemistry 50 Qs each (1 mark each)
  • PCBBiology 50 Qs (100 marks, 2 each) + Physics & Chemistry
  • Weightage20% Class 11 · 80% Class 12 (Maharashtra State Board)
  • DifficultyComparable to JEE Main / NEET

What changes your strategy

No negative marking flips the rule — never leave a question blank. And with 80% from Class 12, weight your revision heavily toward Class 12 while keeping Class 11 fundamentals solid.

Watch the syllabus edits: recently added topics include vectors & error analysis and conic sections; some Class 11 topics were removed. [UPDATE YEARLY]

09 — The part that changes outcomes

How to prepare.

A Class 11 beginner and a dropper need almost opposite advice. Start from where you actually are.

Class 11
2-year plan
  1. Build concepts first — don't rush to PYQs.
  2. Finish NCERT alongside school.
  3. Study 11 & 12 topics in parallel where they connect.
  4. Start light weekly tests by year-end.
Class 12
1-year plan
  1. Balance boards with entrance prep — they overlap.
  2. Move to full-length timed mocks.
  3. Build an error notebook; revise it weekly.
  4. Lock a fixed revision cycle for Class 11 topics.
Dropper
1-year intensive
  1. Diagnose weak areas first — don't restart from zero.
  2. Mocks + deep analysis are your main engine.
  3. Fix the mistakes that cost you last time.
  4. Protect routine and mental health.

Do

  • Finish NCERT before "advanced" books — especially Chemistry & Biology.
  • Keep an error notebook and a one-page formula sheet.
  • Take full-length timed mocks weekly — and analyse them.
  • Revise on a fixed cycle; spaced revision beats re-reading.
  • Study Class 11 and 12 in parallel, not one after the other.
  • Attack high-weightage chapters first.
  • Sleep 7–8 hours. Protect it like a subject.

Don't

  • Don't hoard books or hop between materials.
  • Don't skip revision to "cover more."
  • Don't judge a mock by its score — judge it by mistakes fixed.
  • Don't neglect a subject you dislike; one weak subject sinks the rank.
  • Don't blindly copy a topper's timetable.
  • Don't cram brand-new topics in the last week.
  • Don't ignore the early signs of burnout.

Common mistakes — and the fix

Memorising formulas without understanding them.

Fix: Learn where each formula comes from — you'll recall it under pressure and adapt it to twisted questions.

Solving endless questions but never reviewing the wrong ones.

Fix: Every wrong answer goes in an error notebook. Re-solve it a week later.

Chasing the hardest problems, ignoring easy scoring chapters.

Fix: Secure the high-yield, high-certainty marks first. Hard problems are the last 10%.

No time strategy in the exam hall.

Fix: Practise attempt-order and per-section timing in every mock, not just on exam day.

Careless guessing where there's negative marking — or under-attempting MHT-CET, where there isn't.

Fix: Know each exam's marking maths. In MHT-CET, leave nothing blank.

Passive studying — reading and highlighting instead of recalling.

Fix: Close the book and write what you remember. Active recall is what sticks.

Subject-wise strategy

Physics

Concept-first. Derive, don't memorise. Practise numericals daily until setup becomes instinct.

Chemistry

NCERT for Inorganic, mechanisms for Organic, formula-drill for Physical. Three different games.

Maths

Speed & accuracy through repetition. One chapter, many problems, until it's automatic.

Biology

NCERT line-by-line, diagrams, and memory hooks for classification. Coverage beats cleverness.

On exam day

  • Plan attempt order and time per section in advance.
  • Know when to guess (do the negative-marking maths).
  • NEET is OMR — practise clean, correct bubbling; it costs real marks.
  • Carry admit card + ID; check allowed items the night before. [UPDATE YEARLY]

Look after yourself

Pressure, comparison and expectation are part of this year — but your worth is not your rank. Build in real breaks, keep one thing outside study, and talk to someone you trust when it gets heavy.

11 — Quick answers

Frequently asked.

Can I prepare for JEE, NEET and MHT-CET together?

Partly. Physics and Chemistry overlap across all three, so that effort compounds. The split is Maths (JEE + MHT-CET PCM) vs Biology (NEET + MHT-CET PCB) — you can't seriously chase both at once. Pick your primary goal, and treat MHT-CET as an aligned backup.

Which is hardest?

By competition, NEET (huge applicant pool, few seats) and JEE Advanced (conceptual depth) are toughest. MHT-CET is more scoring — no negative marking, state-board syllabus — but still competitive.

How important is NCERT?

Essential everywhere, decisive for NEET. For NEET Biology and much of Chemistry, NCERT is effectively the exam. Finish it before any other book.

Is coaching necessary?

No — discipline, good material and honest self-testing matter more than any institute. Structure, PYQ practice and consistent revision are what move the rank.

How many mock tests should I take?

Build up to at least one full-length timed mock a week in your final months — and spend as long analysing each one as taking it.
