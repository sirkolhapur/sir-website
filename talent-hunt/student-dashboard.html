SAGE EXAM

Understand. Apply. Reason. Solve.

For Class X, SAGE is a 1-hour multidisciplinary aptitude examination. Questions are based on concepts generally encountered from Classes VI–X, with greater emphasis on using knowledge rather than simply recalling it.

Target: Class X
Duration: 1 hour
Nature: Multidisciplinary aptitude examination
Concept boundary: Classes VI–X
Core emphasis: Conceptual understanding, application, reasoning and problem-solving
QUESTION PHILOSOPHY

Not just “What do you know?”

SAGE can use questions that ask you to understand a concept, apply it to a situation, reason through information, solve a problem, interpret data or visuals, and think through unfamiliar situations where the underlying concept remains within the declared domain.

SAGE EXAM DOMAIN

What can SAGE ask you?

The examination covers five broad domains. The focus is on understanding the underlying concepts and using them intelligently, not on memorising isolated facts.

01 • PHYSICS

Understanding the Physical World

Concepts and areas
  • Measurement, units and estimation
  • Motion, speed, velocity and acceleration
  • Force, friction and Newton's laws
  • Gravitation, pressure and buoyancy
  • Work, energy and power
  • Heat, temperature and heat transfer
  • Sound and waves
  • Reflection, refraction, mirrors and lenses
  • Electricity and simple circuits
  • Magnetism and electromagnetic effects
  • Earth, space and basic astronomy
  • Physical situations, graphs and data

Expect to predict, compare, estimate, interpret and explain.

02 • CHEMISTRY

Understanding Matter & Its Transformation

Concepts and areas
  • Matter and its properties
  • States of matter and changes of state
  • Elements, compounds and mixtures
  • Atoms, molecules and atomic structure
  • Valency and chemical formulae
  • Periodic table and basic trends
  • Chemical reactions and equations
  • Acids, bases and salts
  • Metals and non-metals
  • Carbon compounds
  • Solutions and separation methods
  • Combustion and fuels
  • Everyday chemistry
  • Environment and basic chemical processes

Identify patterns, interpret observations, predict outcomes and apply concepts.

03 • MATHEMATICS

Numbers, Patterns & Problem-Solving

Concepts and areas
  • Number systems, factors and divisibility
  • Fractions, decimals, ratios and percentages
  • Average, profit/loss and interest
  • Speed, distance and time
  • Algebraic expressions and equations
  • Polynomials and factorisation
  • Patterns and sequences
  • Lines, angles and triangles
  • Geometry and geometrical reasoning
  • Circles and quadrilaterals
  • Perimeter, area, surface area and volume
  • Coordinate geometry
  • Graphs and data interpretation
  • Basic probability
  • Mathematical puzzles and non-routine problems
04 • BIOLOGY

Understanding Life

Concepts and areas
  • Diversity and classification
  • Cells and organelles
  • Plant and animal tissues
  • Plant structure and functions
  • Photosynthesis and respiration
  • Nutrition and digestion
  • Human respiratory, circulatory and excretory systems
  • Nervous system, sense organs and coordination
  • Reproduction
  • Microorganisms and diseases
  • Heredity, variation and basic genetics
  • Adaptation and evolution
  • Ecosystems, food chains and food webs
  • Environment and biodiversity
05 • REASONING & APTITUDE

How Do You Think?

Concepts and areas
  • Number and figure series
  • Analogies and classification
  • Pattern recognition
  • Coding and decoding
  • Logical sequences
  • Direction and spatial reasoning
  • Ranking and arrangements
  • Blood relations
  • Venn diagrams
  • Data interpretation
  • Visual reasoning
  • Mathematical puzzles
  • Analytical and critical thinking
  • Unfamiliar problem situations
